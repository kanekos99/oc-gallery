A nine-tailed fox spirit who serves as an advisor in the current Dragon Emperor's court, although his relationship with the emperor seems to be much closer than he lets on. His youthful appearance and penchant for mischief belies his true age and wisdom.

Often serving as the voice of reason for the newly instated emperor, Qiu Ye holds a highly respected position within the court. Yet rumors of his tumultuous past, coupled with longstanding prejudice against his kind, sully his reputation.

While his abilities and loyalty are indisputable, many deem him to be undeserving of being a close aide to the emperor. Qiu Ye himself pays no heed to such dissent. After all, even if the emperor should find his presence to be undesirable one day, he would be content to simply retire to the countryside and become a fisherman.

  • Demonic energy manipulation: As a nine-tailed fox spirit, Qiu Ye can command demonic energy to inflict physical harm upon his opponents. At the peak of his power, he can summon/ transform into a large demonic fox entity. However, he only uses this power when absolutely necessary.
  • Wind magic: He has a good grasp of wind magic and frequently uses wind spells, allowing him to float and fly for short bursts, as well as to make barriers out of wind.
  • Minor talisman spells: He can craft or use pre-made talismans to cast minor spells, such as lighting a fire.
  • Hair pins: He has a set of hair pins that he can throw at his opponents to strike their acupuncture points and immoblise them.
  • Cursed cinnabar blade: Qiu Ye wields an infamous weapon known as the cursed cinnabar blade, which is said to inflict madness upon anyone who tries to draw it out of its sheath other than its rightful owner. It can attack autonomously at his command, and it traps the soul of anyone slain by it. Qiu Ye can also fly around on it. (On a side note, the blade is named such because of its reddish color and not its material, which appears to be steel.)
  • Martial arts: While he mostly relies on his blade or his magic to do his fighting, Qiu Ye also has some level of martial arts training. However, he only knows how to use swords and prefers to use single-edged blades.

  • He likes tofu and all tofu-related food. His favourite is spicy mapo tofu.
  • He has nine tails, but doesn't always show all of them since they take up too much space.
  • Qiu Ye claims to have acquired his cursed sword by fishing it out a pond.

In the mountain ranges of Yueling, an abandoned fox pup was picked up by the old master of the Jin Gui Sect. The young pup's parents had met a grisly fate under the blades of the bandits that haunted the area, while the pup itself had simply been left behind to die on its own.

The old master brought the pup with him back to his sect, situated on an island in the centre of the Golden Turtle Lake, a secluded no man's land. There, he raised the pup among his own disciples, of which there were only three. Despite the illustrious reputation of his sect and his own accomplishments as a practitioner of the mystic arts, the old master was notoriously selective of his students.

The fox pup was bestowed the name Qiu Ye, or “autumn leaf”, in accordance to the naming customs of his race, as well as the season during which he had been found. Although he was kept fed and clothed, the little fox boy was not treated the same as the other disciples. The old master taught him how to read and write, but saw no purpose training him extensively in martial arts or spiritual magic for he was merely a weak fox spirit.

In fact, the old master intended to give Qiu Ye to his disciples as a vessel to enhance their spiritual powers, once the young fox spirit was grown.

As Qiu Ye grew up, he remained none the wiser of the fate his master had planned for him. While he believed himself to be good friends with the other disciples, he was really no more than a mere servant. He was not allowed to attend lessons together with them and most of his time was occupied by countless chores, from cooking and cleaning to gathering firewood in the dead of winter. But the naïve little fox spirit did it all without any complaint, believing that his master and his martial siblings still regarded him kindly.

Shortly after Qiu Ye's eighteenth birthday, the old master sent his disciples to retrieve a legendary spiritual weapon, with Qiu Ye accompanying them as an attendant.
